# What Is AIO? How It Differs from SEO and How to Write Articles AI Can Cite

Alongside SEO, the term **AIO (AI Optimization)** is appearing more often. The basic idea is to make information easy for AI-powered search and answer systems to understand and reference.

Traditional SEO focuses on helping people discover a page through search results. AIO asks an additional question: when an AI system assembles an answer, can it identify a useful, well-supported passage in your article?

## What does AIO mean?

Terminology is still evolving. Related labels include GEO (Generative Engine Optimization), AEO (Answer Engine Optimization), and LLMO (Large Language Model Optimization). They are not perfectly interchangeable, but all concern how content appears in AI-mediated discovery.

AIO should not mean writing for a crawler instead of a human. Good information serves both: clear claims, explicit conditions, useful examples, and evidence readers can inspect.

## SEO and AIO: different goals, shared foundations

| | SEO | AIO |
|---|---|---|
| Primary goal | Visibility and clicks in search results | Being useful as a source in generated answers |
| Emphasis | Search intent, titles, links, site quality | Clear answers, evidence, specific observations |
| Useful unit | Pages and sites | Sometimes a specific passage or test result |

These goals reinforce each other. Clear structure and trustworthy information help readers regardless of how they find the article.

## Three features of especially useful source material

1. **A clear conclusion.** Tell the reader what happened or what works.
2. **A reason or supporting evidence.** Explain why the conclusion is justified.
3. **Specific conditions.** Include device, OS, browser version, code changes, and observed results when relevant.

Compare “PWAs are convenient” with a record of what happened when `display: fullscreen` was tested on a particular Android and iPhone configuration. The second statement provides something another developer can evaluate or reproduce.

## Why hands-on experiment logs matter

At PWA LAB, many interesting results come from behavior that documentation alone does not make obvious: icon cropping, installation differences, Manifest interpretation, and cache updates.

An experiment log becomes useful when it distinguishes the environment, procedure, observation, and interpretation. That is valuable original material whether the reader is a person or an AI system.

## A practical article structure

- **Conclusion:** What worked, failed, or changed?
- **Reason:** What explains the result, and what remains uncertain?
- **Test:** What device, browser, code, and procedure were used?
- **Caveat:** Where might the result differ?
- **Action:** What should another developer try?

Avoid vague “it depends” conclusions without explaining the conditions. Equally, avoid overconfident claims that extend one test result to every browser.

## AIO checklist

Use descriptive titles and headings. Answer the main question early. Identify test environments and dates. Include original code or screenshots when they support a claim. Make tables and examples readable. Ensure pages intended for discovery can actually be crawled. Connect related foundational articles and experiment reports with useful internal links.

None of these steps guarantees an AI citation. The point is to publish information that deserves to be referenced.

## Conclusion

In an AI-assisted search world, original firsthand work matters more, not less. Write the code, run the test, document the surprising behavior, and explain the fix. That produces something genuinely useful for both human readers and answer systems.

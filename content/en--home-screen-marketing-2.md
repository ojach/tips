SEO, social media, advertising, email.

Traditional web marketing has primarily focused on **bringing users to a website**.

Of course, that is still important.

But once a visitor closes the browser, their connection with the website is temporarily lost.

To return, they need to take another action, such as:

- Search for the site again
- Find a bookmark
- Open a social media platform
- Remember the URL

I believe there is another option.

I call it **Home Screen Marketing**.



## The Home Screen Is One of the Most Visible Places

What screen do people see most often on their smartphones?

Social media?

The browser?

No.

It is the home screen.

It appears every time you open an app and may be seen dozens of times every day.

And yet, most websites make almost no use of this space.



## Deliver the “Page,” Not the Website

Traditional PWAs generally add an entire website to the home screen as a single app.

For example, an e-commerce site might create one icon:

```text
Shop
```

But the page a user repeatedly visits may not actually be the shop's homepage.

It could be:

- A favorite product
- An article they frequently read
- A booking page
- A members-only page
- A web tool

In other words, it is often a **specific page**.

That led me to think differently.

Instead of:

“Deliver the website.”

I started thinking:

**“Deliver the page.”**



## The Home Screen Becomes a New Entry Point

For a blog, the home screen could contain a favorite article.

For an e-commerce site:

A product the user wants to buy someday.
A product they purchase every month.

For a restaurant:

The lunch menu or booking page.

For a salon:

The reservation page.

For a SaaS product:

The dashboard.

Users can open those specific pages directly from their home screen.

In other words, your content can exist in a place that is even closer to the user than search results.

## Each Page Can Have Its Own Icon

If individual pages can be added to the home screen, their icons can also be different.

For example:

- Product images
- Featured images
- Brand colors
- Service logos

Each icon can represent the content of its specific page.

Instead of seeing multiple identical website icons on the home screen, users see **icons that each have their own meaning**.

This is an approach that has rarely been associated with traditional PWAs.

## What Types of Websites Are a Good Fit for Home Screen Marketing?

I believe Home Screen Marketing works particularly well with:

**pages that users open repeatedly.**

For example:

- Blogs
- Product pages
- Web tools
- Digital business cards
- Member pages
- Coupon pages
- Event pages
- Landing pages
- Booking pages

The more value an individual page has on its own, the more useful this approach can become.
